The First Day

The first day.

I took the subway to the station with two bags and a bicycle. The train carried me west for eleven hours, and then the city was gone.

I have lived the same lifestyle for sixteen years: office, subway, office, phone. Faster and faster, and I could not say what for. People call that pace normal. I call it a habit I did not choose.

Now the road is simple. In front of me: rural land, small towns, and then the mountains. I will not see a big city again for two months.

Tomorrow I start hiking. Not running — walking. My body will decide how far.

People ask why I do this. There is no single reason, and any answer I give would be a part of it. The views will be diverse — dry land, green hills, snow. The days will have more variety than my whole last year.

Leisure used to mean a free afternoon. Now it means the whole day. I want to see how far this road can extend.
